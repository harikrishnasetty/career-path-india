Three questions every Class 10 student has

You are not the only one who is confused. Almost every student asks these same three questions after the board exams — here are the straight answers.

"Which stream is best for me?"

No stream is the "best" — only the one that fits your interests, strengths and goals. Science keeps the widest options open, Commerce builds finance & business careers, and Arts now offers the largest variety of career paths (law, UPSC, design, psychology, media and more).

"Which exams do I need to write?"

In Class 9 and 10 you mainly write scholarships and Olympiads. The big entrance exams (JEE, NEET, CUET, NDA, CLAT, NIFT…) come after Class 12 — but you must choose subjects in Class 11 keeping those exams in mind.

"What job will I get?"

Each stream leads to specific jobs, sectors and salary ranges. Some routes let you start earning in 1–2 years (ITI, diploma), others take longer but lead to professions like doctor, engineer, lawyer or civil servant.

What changed in 2026

Three big updates every student must know

Indian school education changed significantly in 2026. If you are in Class 9 or 10 right now, these directly affect you.

Board exams

CBSE Class 10 boards are now held twice a year

From 2026, the first board exam is in February (compulsory) and an optional second exam in May to improve up to three subjects (Science, Maths, Social Science, languages). Results come in April and June — the better score is kept. Internal assessments happen only once.

Read the full explanation →
Scholarships

KVPY is gone — INSPIRE-SHE replaces it

KVPY (Kishore Vaigyanik Protsahan Yojana) was permanently discontinued in 2022. Its benefits now sit inside INSPIRE-SHE (about ₹80,000 per year for science students). Never search for "KVPY form" — it no longer exists.

Talent tests

NTSE has been on hold since 2021

The National Talent Search Examination has not been conducted since 2021. In August 2026 a Parliamentary committee called the suspension a "severe blow to the talent pipeline" and pushed for revival. Keep checking the NCERT site; meanwhile, Olympiads and NSTSE continue.

FAQ

Straight answers to the questions students ask most

Which stream should I choose after 10th in India?

There is no single "best" stream — only what fits you. Start with three filters:

  • Subjects you genuinely enjoy (not what your friends chose) — if you like solving maths problems, PCM makes life easier; if you love understanding living things, PCB fits.
  • The jobs you find interesting — picture a normal working day you would not hate.
  • How you like to learn — heavy theory and long study (medicine, law) vs hands-on practical work (diploma, ITI).

Remember: your stream does not permanently decide your career. Arts students become managers, science students become civil servants, and diploma holders become B.Tech engineers through lateral entry.

What are the career options after 10th in India?

Six broad routes, all covered in detail on this site:

  • Science PCM — engineering, architecture, IT, data science, defence, aviation, research
  • Science PCB — MBBS, BDS, nursing, pharmacy, biotech, veterinary, agriculture
  • Commerce — CA, CS, CMA, B.Com, BBA, banking, finance, entrepreneurship
  • Arts / Humanities — law, UPSC civil services, journalism, design, psychology, teaching
  • Polytechnic diploma (3 years after 10th) — junior engineer roles, apprenticeships, B.Tech lateral entry
  • ITI / vocational courses — electrician, mechanic, welder, computer operator and other skilled trades with early employability
Which exams do I write after Class 10?

Right now (Class 9–10): National Scholarship Portal scholarships, Olympiads, NSTSE, and NTSE if/when NCERT revives it.

After Class 10 results (admission season): state polytechnic entrances (for example JEECUP in UP, POLYCET in AP/TS), ITI admissions through state portals, and Class 11 admission based on board marks.

From Class 11–12 (prepare now, write later): JEE Main/Advanced, NEET UG, CUET UG, NDA, CLAT, NIFT, NID, UCEED, NATA, IPMAT, BITSAT, state CETs. These all need 10+2, so your Class 11 subject choice must keep the relevant exam open.

Is CBSE conducting the Class 10 board exam twice a year from 2026?

Yes. Under NEP 2020, CBSE now runs two Class 10 board exams per year: a compulsory February exam and an optional May improvement exam. You can improve up to three subjects (Science, Mathematics, Social Science and languages). Results arrive in April and June, the better score of the two is retained, and internal assessments happen once before the first exam. The fee for both exams is collected up front at application time.

Is NTSE still there? What happened to KVPY?

NTSE: on hold since 2021. A Parliamentary Standing Committee highlighted in August 2026 that its suspension severely hurts the talent pipeline and urged revival — so keep watching ncert.nic.in.

KVPY: permanently discontinued in 2022; merged into INSPIRE-SHE (Department of Science and Technology), which gives around ₹80,000 per year to eligible science students based on board/competitive merit instead of a separate aptitude test.

Can I switch streams later if I pick the wrong one?

Yes, in most situations. Most boards allow a change after Class 11 subject to minimum marks; NIOS open schooling lets you restructure subjects; many career routes accept any stream after Class 12 (law via CLAT, design via NIFT/NID, NDA Army wing, hotel management, civil services, most BA/BBA/B.Com degrees). Diploma and ITI routes also allow later entry to B.Tech or graduation. One decision at age 15 never closes every door.

Which stream has the most career options?

Science keeps the widest set of technical and medical pathways open (roughly 35–40 distinct career directions). Commerce maps to 30+ (finance, business, professional courses), and Arts/Humanities actually offers the widest variety overall — 40+ directions spanning law, civil services, media, design, psychology, languages and the social sciences. The better question is: which stream keeps your best options open?

What can I do after 10th if I don't want to study further right now?

You still have respectable options: ITI trades (6 months–2 years, then apprenticeships and skilled jobs), polytechnic diploma (3 years, junior engineer roles), vocational/skill courses under NSDC partners, and defence entries like Agniveer (eligibility varies by trade — Class 10 for some). You can also continue studies through NIOS while doing a course or job. Always keep some form of education going alongside — it multiplies your options later.
