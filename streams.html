Every stream after Class 10 — explained simply

Science, Commerce, Arts, Agriculture, Polytechnic diploma, ITI and vocational courses: what you study, what you learn, which exams you need, where it leads and how much it can pay. Read the one that interests you, or compare all of them in the table at the bottom.

?

Before you pick: the 4-question filter

1) Which subjects do I genuinely enjoy?  2) Which kind of work would I not hate every day?  3) Do I prefer theory and long study, or hands-on practical work?  4) Do I need to start earning early, or can I study for 5–7 more years? Your honest answers matter more than anyone's opinion.

✓

Remember: no stream permanently locks any door

Arts students become managers and judges, science students become civil servants and designers, commerce students become lawyers and data analysts. Diploma and ITI students graduate to B.Tech and degrees later. Choose carefully — but not fearfully.

!

Check availability before you decide

Not every school offers every stream. Before locking in PCM/PCB/PCMB or subject combinations like Psychology or Agriculture, confirm with your school (or the schools you may join) which combinations exist, and whether Maths/Biology is compulsory in Class 11.

SCI

Science Stream

Three combinations: PCM, PCB and PCMB — the route with the widest technical & medical options
Admission after 10thSchool/board merit (11th admission); most states have no entrance test
Big exams after 12JEE Main/Advanced, NEET UG, CUET, NDA, NATA, state CETs
Typical course length2 yrs (11–12) + 3–5.5 yrs graduation/professional course
Indicative starting pay₹3.5 – 12 LPA (varies hugely by role & institute)

PCM — what you take

  • Physics
  • Chemistry
  • Mathematics (with calculus, algebra, coordinate geometry, probability)
  • English (core language)
  • 5th subject options: Computer Science, Informatics Practices, Physical Education, Psychology, Engineering Graphics

What you actually learn

  • Logical and mathematical problem solving (the #1 skill for tech careers)
  • How the physical world works — motion, energy, electronics, materials
  • Programming basics if you take Computer Science (Python/SQL in CBSE)
  • Scientific temper: hypothesis, data, experiment

Pick PCM if…

  • You enjoy maths and don't mind heavy numerical work
  • You like building, machines, computers, gadgets
  • You want engineering, architecture, data science, IT, defence (Air Force/Navy) or aviation later

Watch out: if you dislike maths, PCM becomes a daily struggle — do not pick it for "scope" alone.

PCB — what you take

  • Physics
  • Chemistry
  • Biology (botany + zoology)
  • English
  • 5th subject options: Psychology, Maths, Computer Science, Home Science

What you actually learn

  • How living bodies work — from cells to human physiology
  • Chemistry of drugs, food, environment
  • Lab skills: microscopy, dissection, experiments
  • Patience for long, detail-heavy study — essential for medicine

Pick PCB if…

  • You want to be a doctor, dentist, pharmacist, nurse, vet, or researcher
  • You find the human body, plants, health and life sciences fascinating
  • You are prepared for intense competition (NEET) and 5.5+ years of study for MBBS

Watch out: NEET is extremely competitive — keep allied-health options (pharmacy, nursing, physiotherapy, BSc) ready as strong backups.

PCMB — what you take

  • Physics + Chemistry + Maths + Biology together
  • Plus English and one 5th subject
  • Available in most CBSE/ICSE and many state schools (confirm first)

Why students choose it

  • Keeps both JEE and NEET doors open until Class 12
  • Useful for biotech, bioinformatics, food tech, forensic science, research
  • Optionality feels safer when you are undecided

Honest reality check

  • The heaviest workload of all combinations — four lab-heavy subjects
  • Needs strong time management from Day 1 of Class 11
  • Best only if you like both maths and biology — not as a hedge

Watch out: many students pick PCMB out of confusion, then underperform in all four. Decide with your Class 9/10 performance as evidence.

Where Science leads — the main exam map

Engineering/Tech: JEE Main & Advanced, BITSAT, state CETs (MHT-CET, WBJEE, KCET, AP EAPCET, TS EAMCET…)  •  Medicine: NEET UG  •  Architecture: NATA / JEE Paper 2  •  Defence: NDA (Air Force & Navy need PCM)  •  Research: IISER via JEE/NEET/SCB, INSPIRE-SHE scholarship after 12  •  Universities: CUET UG. COM

Commerce Stream

Money, business, markets and management — the route to CA, finance, banking and entrepreneurship
Admission after 10thSchool/board merit; Maths in Class 10 often decides "with Maths" option
Big exams after 12CA Foundation, CSEET (CS), CMA Foundation, CUET, IPMAT, NPAT, state CETs
Typical course length2 yrs (11–12) + 3 yrs graduation or 3–5 yrs professional courses
Indicative starting pay₹3 – 10 LPA (CA/MBA tops much higher with experience)

Subjects — with Maths

  • Accountancy (financial accounts, company accounts)
  • Business Studies
  • Economics (micro + macro + Indian economic development)
  • Mathematics (applied maths/statistics)
  • 5th subject: Informatics Practices, Entrepreneurship, Legal Studies, English core

Subjects — without Maths

  • Accountancy, Business Studies, Economics (same core)
  • English + 5th subject (Informatics Practices / Entrepreneurship / Legal Studies)
  • Tip: keeping Maths (or Applied Maths) opens B.Com Hons, economics, actuarial science and finance-heavy MBA roles later

What you actually learn

  • How money flows in a business: ledger, balance sheet, profit & loss
  • How markets, taxes, banking and the economy work
  • Business communication and entrepreneurial thinking
  • Spreadsheet/data skills if you take Informatics Practices

Pick Commerce if…

  • You enjoy numbers, money, business and economics
  • You like the idea of CA/CS/CMA, banking, finance, stock markets or startups
  • You want a professional qualification where skills matter more than college brand
  • You prefer practical, real-world study over abstract theory

Where it leads — careers

  • Chartered Accountant (CA), Company Secretary (CS), Cost Accountant (CMA)
  • Banking (IBPS/SBI/RBI), financial analysis, investment, insurance
  • B.Com / BBA / BA Economics → MBA, digital marketing, business analytics
  • Actuarial science (if you keep Maths), entrepreneurship

Watch out for

  • CA/CS/CMA have tough, multi-level exams — dropout rates are real; start early and stay disciplined
  • Commerce without Maths narrows some finance/economics routes
  • "Commerce = easy" is a myth; top finance roles want strong maths + communication
✓

Class 11 Commerce + Coding is a powerful combo

Commerce students who add coding/spreadsheets/data skills (via Informatics Practices, free courses, or self-study) move easily into business analytics, fintech and product roles — some of the best-paying non-engineering jobs in India.

ART

Arts / Humanities Stream

The most diverse stream — law, civil services, media, design, psychology, teaching and the social sciences
Admission after 10thSchool/board merit; widest subject freedom of any stream
Big exams after 12CLAT/AILET (law), CUET, NIFT, NID, UCEED, NCHM JEE, UPSC (after graduation)
Typical course length2 yrs (11–12) + 3–5 yrs (BA/BA Hons/5-yr integrated law/design)
Indicative starting pay₹3 – 10 LPA (law, corporate comms, UX & UPSC roles lead high)

Popular subject combinations

  • Law-minded: Political Science + History + Economics + Legal Studies
  • Psychology-minded: Psychology + Sociology + English (+ Biology if school allows)
  • UPSC-minded: History, Pol Science, Geography, Economics, Sociology
  • Creative: Fine Arts, Media Studies, Journalism, Home Science, Psychology
  • Language combos: English + a foreign language (French/German/Spanish — high global demand)

What you actually learn

  • Reading deeply, writing clearly, arguing with evidence — the core skills for law, media and civil services
  • How societies, governments and economies behave
  • Human mind and behaviour (psychology), design thinking if you take arts subjects
  • Research, debate, presentation and independent thought

Pick Arts if…

  • You love reading, writing, debating or understanding people and society
  • You dream of law, civil services (IAS/IPS), journalism, psychology or design
  • You are creative — drawing, storytelling, film, social media, teaching
  • You want freedom to combine subjects (e.g., Psychology + Economics + English)

Watch out: you must drive your own preparation for CLAT/UPSC/NIFT from Class 11 — Arts rewards self-starters.

Careers Arts leads to

  • Lawyer / corporate lawyer / legal advisor (CLAT → BA/BBA/BCom LLB)
  • IAS, IPS, IFS and other civil services (UPSC after graduation)
  • Journalist, PR, advertising, content strategy
  • Psychologist / counselor (BA Psychology → MA/M.Sc)
  • Designer: fashion (NIFT), communication/UX (NID, UCEED, private)
  • Teacher/professor, social worker, public policy, foreign languages

Big exams for Arts students

  • CLAT — 5-yr law after 12 (any stream)
  • CUET UG — central & many private universities
  • NIFT / NID / UCEED — design (any stream after 12)
  • NCHM JEE — hotel management
  • UPSC CSE — after any graduation
  • BA entrances of individual universities (IIMC, SET, CUET-PG later)

Money reality

  • Starting salaries vary enormously: media/NGO start modest (₹3–5 LPA), corporate law, UX and product roles can start ₹6–12+ LPA
  • UPSC, PSU and teaching jobs add stability and benefits
  • Skills + portfolio beat degree brand — build writing, design or case prep alongside
DIP

Polytechnic Diploma (3 years after 10th)

Hands-on engineering training straight after Class 10 — with a direct path to jobs, apprenticeships and B.Tech lateral entry
EligibilityClass 10 pass (no age bar for most state diplomas)
Admission routeState polytechnic entrance exams or 10th-mark merit (varies by state)
Duration3 years full-time (2 years lateral entry to B.Tech in year 2)
Indicative starting pay₹2.5 – 6 LPA (fresher); higher with govt JE jobs or B.Tech later

Popular diploma branches

  • Civil Engineering
  • Mechanical Engineering
  • Electrical Engineering
  • Computer Science & Engineering
  • Electronics & Communication
  • Automobile, Chemical, IT, Instrumentation (state-dependent)

What you actually learn

  • Workshop and lab skills from semester 1 — drafting, machining, circuits, coding
  • Site visits, industrial training and project work
  • The applied side of the same engineering subjects B.Tech students study in theory

Diploma is regulated by AICTE; certificates come from state boards of technical education (SBTEs) or polytechnic councils.

Where it leads

  • Jobs: junior engineer/technician roles in government (state PWD, railways, electricity boards) and private industry
  • Higher study: lateral entry into B.Tech (2nd year) in the same branch — many state universities and private universities allow it
  • Apprenticeships: under the Apprentices Act with stipends in PSUs and companies
  • Self-employment: fabrication, electrical, civil contracting work

Pick diploma if…

  • You like practical, machine/computer work more than long theory books
  • You want a job or earning route without first doing 11th & 12th
  • You want a safety net: diploma still lets you do B.Tech later

Watch out for

  • Institute quality varies hugely — check AICTE approval and placement records before joining
  • If your final goal is a top IIT/NIT, class 12 + JEE remains the standard route
  • Some government JE posts need the right branch + category; verify the latest notification

Diploma vs 11th–12th vs ITI

  • Diploma: 3 years, technical qualification, B.Tech lateral option
  • 11th–12th: 2 years, keeps all graduate routes open
  • ITI: 6 months–2 yrs, trade skill, fastest to earning, plus apprenticeship

ITI

ITI & Vocational Courses

Skill-first routes — 6 months to 2 years, fastest employability in India, with clear upgrade paths to diplomas and degrees
EligibilityClass 10 pass for most trades (some trades admit after Class 8)
Admission routeState ITI admission portals / merit (no single national test)
Duration6 months – 2 years (NCVT/SCVT certificate, All India Trade Test)
Indicative starting pay₹1.8 – 4 LPA initially; rises with skill, experience & govt jobs

Popular ITI trades

  • Electrician
  • Fitter / Turner / Machinist
  • Mechanic (Motor Vehicle / Diesel / Refrigeration & AC)
  • Welder
  • COPA (Computer Operator & Programming Assistant)
  • Plumber, Draughtsman (Civil/Mechanical), Instrument Mechanic
  • Cookery/Health Sanitary Inspector/Basic Cosmetology (service trades)

What you actually learn

  • Real workshop skills employers test on Day 1
  • Safety procedures, blueprint reading, tool handling, maintenance
  • Computer fundamentals (in COPA and several trades)
  • On-the-job experience through compulsory training/apprenticeships

Where it leads

  • Apprenticeship in PSUs and private companies (monthly stipend under the Apprentices Act)
  • Government: industrial/technical posts, defence (Agniveer technical trades), railways
  • Private: maintenance technician, machine operator, service technician roles
  • Upgrade: lateral entry to diploma (via NATS/NAC), and later degree programs
  • Own work: electrical, plumbing, fabrication, AC repair businesses

Pick ITI if…

  • You want to start earning within 1–2 years
  • You learn better by doing than by reading
  • You want a quick, concrete skill with self-employment options

Vocational courses (the broader family)

  • NSQF-aligned skill courses in IT-ITeS, healthcare, tourism, retail, beauty & wellness, electronics
  • Polytechnic "vocational" diplomas and Craft Teacher Training
  • Short courses (3–12 months) from government and private skill providers

Watch out for

  • Only join recognized ITIs (NCVT/SCVT) or AICTE-approved institutes — avoid random "institutes" with unverifiable certificates
  • ITI alone gives modest starting pay — combine it with apprenticeship, diploma or upgrades for growth
  • Check the trade's current demand in your state before choosing
OTH

Other important routes

Agriculture stream, NIOS open schooling, defence entries, sports and short skill courses

Agriculture stream (Class 11)

  • Many boards and state schools now offer Agriculture as a Class 11 subject (with Science combinations) — covering agronomy, soil science, animal husbandry, biotech
  • Leads to B.Sc Agriculture / B.Sc Horticulture / B.F.Sc — a strong government-recruitment sector (agriculture officer, research, agri-business)
  • Admissions via ICAR/CUET and state agricultural university entrances — check the current mode each year
  • Agriculture is a genuine "sector of its own": food companies, agri-tech startups, banks (agri officer), research

NIOS (open schooling)

  • National Institute of Open Schooling — government open school for Class 10 & 12 (and more)
  • Useful if you want to: appear for boards with a flexible schedule, re-appear to improve marks, or study alongside a job/sport/arts training
  • NIOS certificates are recognized for higher education on par with other boards — many universities accept them
  • Registration is online via nios.ac.in

Defence & armed forces

  • Sainik Schools (AISSEE): Class 9 entry — apply while in Class 8; ages 13–15 as on 31 March; NTA conducts the January exam
  • Agniveer: after Class 10/12 depending on trade (GD trades take Class 10; many technical trades need 10+2)
  • NDA: after Class 12 (Army: any stream; Air Force & Navy: PCM), age roughly 16.5–19.5 years
  • Military schools like RIMC and Rashtriya Military School have their own entry exams — check each school's notification

Sports route

  • Sports quota admissions in colleges and universities (state/national level certificates)
  • Sports Authority of India (SAI) and national sports federations' training centers
  • scholarships for sportspersons (central and state)
  • Important: keep a mainstream education running alongside — most athletes need a second career too

Short skill courses

  • Digital skills: web development, data entry, digital marketing, graphic design (3–12 months)
  • Service skills: baking, tailoring, mobile repair, beauty, electric vehicle servicing
  • Government skill programs under Skill India/NSDC partners — verify NSQF alignment
  • These can be done alongside Class 11–12 in many cases

Work right after 10th?

  • Legally you can work, but child-labour law restricts work below age 14, and most good jobs need at least ITI/10+2
  • Government openings for Class 10 pass candidates exist (e.g., SSC GD constable, some railway/industrial posts, Agniveer GD) but competition is heavy
  • Strong advice: if you must earn early, do it through ITI/apprenticeship while continuing studies via NIOS or open degree programs

Myth vs reality

5 myths that ruin stream decisions

✗

"Arts has no scope"

Reality: Arts offers the widest variety of career paths — law, UPSC, design, psychology, media, languages, policy. Corporate law, UX design and civil services are among India's most respected careers.

✗

"Only PCM students can do computer jobs"

Reality: software and data careers care about skill. Commerce and Arts students enter via BCA, BSc CS, bootcamps and self-learning. Computer Science in Class 11 helps but is not mandatory.

✗

"Science means doctor or engineer only"

Reality: science also leads to architecture, forensic science, food technology, environmental science, psychology, statistics, data science, pilots, research and more.

✗

"Diploma/ITI is for weak students"

Reality: these are skill-based routes with real demand. Diploma holders become engineers via lateral entry; ITI trades feed defence, railways, PSUs and well-paid private service jobs.

✗

"Choosing the wrong stream ends my career"

Reality: streams can be changed (most boards allow it after Class 11 with minimum marks), NIOS lets you restructure subjects, and most graduate entrances accept any stream. The real risk is not choosing at all — or choosing under peer/parent pressure and then switching off mentally.
